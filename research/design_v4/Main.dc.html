ctrl ctrl-free Design research v1 · sprintd Zurich · Equans case · 9 Oct 2026

From a 202-page tender to a first price an estimator trusts and can explain.

Today an estimator reads the tender, hunts for products and hours with Ctrl+F, and adds it all up in Excel. ctrl-free does the reading and the arithmetic, shows where every number came from, and leaves the decisions to the person.

The problem today
Hundreds of pages. Conditions, specs and the bill of quantities (LV) in one German PDF. Oberglatt: pages 67–199 are the price list.
Data hunting. Products, supplier prices and install hours sit in separate files and handbooks.
Manual estimates. Labour, install time and costs are added up by hand. Experts gather data instead of judging risk.
Source: Equans case slides, sprintd case page
Who we design for
The estimator
Knows ventilation inside out, is not a software person. Lives in PDF, Excel and email. Has to defend every number to the bid lead.
Needs: "Tell me what needs me, show me the page, let me fix it."
The bid lead
Sets the margin. Today that is gut feel about the competition.
Needs: "Give me options and the trade-off, not one magic number."

Design principles

Five rules every screen follows

01
Ask, don't dump
Open on "6 things need you", sorted by money at risk. The 200-line table is one click away, never the start.
02
Every number is a sentence
"5 valves × CHF 74.80, plus 4% waste = CHF 388.96". No formulas without words.
03
One click to the page
Every line opens its tender page with the source text highlighted. Original German, English underneath.
04
Never fake a price
Unknown lines get a clear label and a next step: ask a supplier, add the quantity, or leave it out.
05
You decide
The tool suggests, you confirm. Any change updates the total at once and is logged with your name.
